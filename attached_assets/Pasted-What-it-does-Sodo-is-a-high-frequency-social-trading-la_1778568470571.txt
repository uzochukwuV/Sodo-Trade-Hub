What it does
Sodo is a high-frequency social trading layer built around real-time Sodex trading activity and trader intelligence.

The platform ingests live trading activity from the Sodex ecosystem, analyzes wallet behavior, computes trader reputation and pnl, and turns every trade into a social event.

Users can:

discover high-performing traders
track live positions and market rotations
follow wallets and narratives
view verified trader performance
see real-time buy/sell activity feeds
post intents and market opinions
interact through comments, reactions, and Pain Room discussions
connect their wallets to analyze their own trading behavior
execute trades directly through Sodex infrastructure
We are also building a Trader Graph that maps:

which wallets behave similarly
which wallets enter together
which wallets rotate narratives together
which wallets lead moves
which wallets follow moves
The platform transforms raw onchain trading activity into a real-time social intelligence network.

The problem it solves
Most trading platforms are fragmented and lack context.

Traders usually see:

price charts
isolated wallets
random signals
delayed analytics
but they cannot understand:

who consistently leads market moves
which traders are worth following
how narratives spread across wallets
how top traders behave in real time
whether a signal comes from actual profitable trading activity
Traditional social trading platforms are also filled with fake pnl screenshots, unverifiable claims, and low-quality signals.

Sodo solves this by building a verified social layer directly on top of real Sodex trading activity.

Every signal, reputation score, and trader profile is tied to real wallet behavior, real positions, and real trading history.

Challenges I ran into
One major challenge was moving from mock trading data to fully real-time exchange-driven data.

Initially, some UI systems and analytics were built using placeholder data while we researched the actual Sodex and ValueChain infrastructure.

We then had to:

reverse engineer explorer behavior
discover working REST and websocket endpoints
map wallet activity to real trading history
build reliable trade verification systems
handle duplicate trade ingestion
synchronize frontend schemas with backend APIs
compute real pnl and win-rate metrics from live positions
Another challenge was that some Sodex APIs expose fills and positions differently from onchain explorer data, so we had to combine multiple data sources to build accurate trader intelligence.

Scaling real-time ingestion and trader graph analytics without producing noisy or fake signals was also a major architectural challenge.

How we built it
We started by integrating live Sodex market data including:

tickers
trades
orderbooks
klines
positions
Then we built:

trader ingestion systems
wallet analytics pipelines
live trade feeds
pnl and win-rate computation
signal verification
reputation scoring systems
Next, we connected real wallet addresses and transaction history to trader profiles so activity could be verified publicly through the explorer.

We then evolved the product into a real-time social trading layer by adding:

feeds
comments
intents
reactions
Pain Room discussions
trader following systems
The newest part of the system is the Trader Graph engine, which analyzes behavioral relationships between wallets and detects coordinated or correlated trading activity.

What we learned
We learned that raw trading activity becomes much more valuable when transformed into social and behavioral intelligence.

Instead of simply showing trades, users care about:

who entered first
who consistently wins
which traders influence narratives
which wallets move together
how market sentiment evolves in real time
We also learned that credibility matters deeply in trading products.

Verified wallet activity, real pnl, and transparent historical performance create far more trust than traditional screenshot-based trading communities.

Another major insight was that social engagement increases significantly when trading activity itself becomes content.

Every trade can become:

a signal
a discussion
a learning opportunity
a behavioral datapoint
a social interaction
